Friedman Economic Games · Proposal 8

Supermarket

Explore queue equilibria, negative feedback, search costs, and imperfect information.

Review prototype · v1. Compare the same shoppers in two stores. The baseline uses nearby people counts; the experiment can pay for basket information or reserve an express lane. Arrivals stay fixed: waiting costs do not change demand in this version.

Try a comparison

  1. Choose a scenario or edit the experiment settings and apply them.
  2. Step through time, Run, or Finish comparison. Both stores receive the same arrivals and baskets.
  3. Compare total exit time, including search and travel. Let every shopper finish before comparing final averages.

Time is measured in abstract units, not minutes. Each basket takes its number of items plus three units to check out. Shoppers choose once and stay in their queue.

Set up the experiment

Only the experiment reserves a lane. Larger baskets use lanes 1–3.

After this duration, arrivals stop and the comparison continues until both stores are empty.

Both stores report cohorts using this basket limit, even when express is off.

Jump to comparison results

Run advances ten units every 700 milliseconds. Pause stops Run or Finish comparison. Motion is a schematic; the tables carry the same results.

The two stores

Each card shows the basket in service and up to six waiting baskets, followed by the remaining count. A workload is the time needed to finish the current line if no one else joins.

Compare total exit time

All averages below are per completed shopper. Search + travel + queue wait + service = total exit time. During playback, unfinished shoppers are excluded and the averages are provisional.

Searching and non-searching groups contain different shoppers. Their averages alone do not isolate the effect of searching. The final all-shopper comparison uses the same arrivals and baskets in both stores.

Individual shopper evidence

The same shopper ID, basket, source, and arrival appear in both stores. Searchers forecast from current basket workloads; they cannot see later arrivals. Non-searchers compare only two nearby people counts.
